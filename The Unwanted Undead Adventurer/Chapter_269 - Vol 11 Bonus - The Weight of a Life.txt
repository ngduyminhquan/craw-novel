Vol 11 Bonus - The Weight of a Life

“Hmm?”

One day, as I was walking to town on my way back from a job, I heard the sound of an animal whining.

Normally, I wouldn’t have paid it any mind. Since it had come from within the forest, chances were that it was most likely an infant monster calling for its parent. If I approached it carelessly, said parent could come charging out of nowhere, landing me in a whole heap of trouble.

I could just slay whatever came for me, of course, but the thought of doing so kind of bothered me. Even monsters had kids. There was no need for me to specifically target them for slaughter. Well, in the case of monsters like goblins and orcs, though, they were basically vermin, so they were no weight on my conscience. However, wolf species of monsters, among others, didn’t always attack humans. Stumbling across one would lead to a fight, sure, but otherwise, they lived quiet lives deep inside forests. I still occasionally went out to hunt them for their pelts, though. Human emotions were complicated like that—not that I was human.

In any case, while I wanted to simply ignore the whine and go on my way, I couldn’t shake it from my thoughts. It sounded almost sad, in a way. Unable to stop myself, I stepped into the trees, and that was when I found it.

“So that’s what that noise was, huh?” I muttered.

Before me was a monster that looked like a puppy—a wolf pup, to be exact. By its side, stretched out on the ground, was a much larger monster, but this one had already died. There was a massive wound in its abdomen. At a guess, another monster had gotten it. Its body looked relatively untouched, if that were the case, but...

“No, I suppose that’s because I got in the way...”

Sensing a presence somewhere in my surroundings, I drew my sword. The monster leaped out at me, swift as the wind, and I turned to meet its assault. As it sailed through the air, I stepped to the side and slashed at it as it passed by. When it crumpled to the ground, I saw that it was a cat species of monster.

It growled at me, but the wound I’d given it was fatal.

“Sorry,” I said. Then, I finished it off.

I couldn’t say that I felt bad for it, not exactly, but I certainly didn’t feel good about what I’d done. But that aside...

“Woof!”

Something bumped against my leg. When I looked down, I saw that the wolf pup was rubbing itself up against me. Its parent had just died, and yet...

“You’re a strong one, aren’t you?” I said. I patted it, and it licked my hand. I felt a little better after that. Then, I had a thought. “Hey, would you like to come home with me? I can’t promise anything luxurious, though.”

“Woof, woof!”

I took that as a yes and picked it up. It was light. Nevertheless, the weight of a life in my arms was unmistakable.

◆◇◆◇◆

The first thing Lorraine said when I brought it back and explained myself was, “So, then you brought it home? I suppose I don’t mind, but you’re not going to keep it forever, are you? What’s your plan?”

Her concerns were only natural. However, I had an idea.

“It’s a black wolf pup,” I said. “Besides, it would’ve had a hard time surviving out there, given how young it is. I think we can find a home for it.”

Black wolves were a species of monster known for the relative ease with which they grew attached to humans. Their pups especially, if raised alongside a human child, could grow to become steadfast companions. That was why many nobles and other wealthy individuals were often on the lookout for one to give to their children.

“That’s certainly logical...” Lorraine said. “It’d have a good life that way too. I suppose I’ll search for an owner who won’t mistreat it, then.”

“Sorry,” I apologized. “I’ll look too, but you’re a lot better with that kind of thing.”

“Mmm, there are a lot of noblewomen who buy my products. But that aside, first we have to sort out the pup’s food.”

“You’re right about that. I’ve been giving it water and mashed-up jerky so far, but milk would probably be better.”

“That’s monsters for you. Most are omnivorous, so even when they’re young they have decent jaw strength. Now then, where did I put that milk...?”

◆◇◆◇◆

Thus, we began living with the wolf pup. It didn’t last long, but it was fun. The pup warmed up to us fast and was extremely intelligent. I’d been concerned that, since it was a monster, some of its inherent savagery would show itself, but my fears were unfounded. In only a week, we had become completely attached to it.

“Are we really letting it go?” Lorraine said, holding the pup to her chest and glaring at me. “I mean, I know Baroness Graham is a lovely woman, and that her son has a good head on his shoulders, and that they’ll definitely love and care for it, but, I just...”

“I get it, I really do,” I said. “But we can’t raise it forever. It’s not like Edel, who ended up as my familiar by accident.”

I didn’t want my unusual circumstances to drag the pup into anything unpleasant, and just from a practical standpoint, black wolves grew to become fairly sizable. Lorraine’s home wasn’t exactly a wide-open estate, and soon enough the poor pup would start feeling cramped. Lorraine already knew all of this, of course.

“I suppose there’s no choice,” she said. She looked down at the pup. “Live well, all right? We’ll come see you one day. I’ve made a promise with the baroness and her son.”

“It’ll probably have forgotten about us by then,” I said.

“I know, I know. That’s why we need to give it away now, while it’s still a pup.” Lorraine sighed. “Okay. I’ll take it over to the baroness now.”

“Yeah.” I patted the little wolf. “Grow up big and strong, all right?”

“Woof...”

The pup looked slightly sad as it licked my hand. That was enough for me.

Lorraine left and took it to its new home. I said goodbye and stayed back to watch the house.

◆◇◆◇◆

“I wish we could’ve named it, at least...” Lorraine said when she returned, a sad look on her face.

“If we did, it would’ve thought of us as its masters,” I said, rubbing her head.

“It’s fine. It’ll have a happy life at its new home.”

Lorraine shed a few tears. “You’re right,” she said after a few moments had passed. “I really hope it will.”

Then, she smiled. It looked like she was trying to hold back the pain.
