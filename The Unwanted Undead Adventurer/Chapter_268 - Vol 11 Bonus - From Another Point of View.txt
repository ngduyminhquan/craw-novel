Vol 11 Bonus - From Another Point of View

“How about an escort job for a change, Rentt?”

One day, back when Augurey had still been in Maalt, he’d come to invite me along on a job—not that his doing so was rare or anything. It wasn’t exactly a daily occurrence, but we were on close enough terms that we worked together whenever the mood struck.

I maintained a reputation for working solo, so others rarely invited me along on jobs, and every now and then, I felt down about that. In times like those, I was grateful for people like Augurey, who came calling without a hint of reserve. Still...

“An escort job, huh?” I asked. “It’d depend on the client.”

If they were some high-handed noble or a merchant who thought they could treat us like slaves just because they paid us, then I wanted no part of it. I knew that people like those were necessary if the guild wanted to function, but that didn’t mean I couldn’t be selective with the work I took on. It might have been a different story if I was desperate for coin, but I didn’t have that particular vice, and currently, there wasn’t anything that required me to dig deep into my purse either.

“It’s a kid,” Augurey said. “He’s a little...different from others his age, from what I hear, but all we need to do is bring him to the next town over. We’ll be over and done with the job in no time.”

“One kid? He’s alone?”

“You got it. We’re taking him to his parents.”

“Why were they separated?” I asked dubiously.

Augurey was quick to reply. “Remember the huge storm we had recently? I think his town suffered a decent bit of damage during it, or something similar, so his parents left him with relatives in Maalt for a while as things recovered.”

“That makes sense. In that case, I can’t see anything questionable about this job...” I thought it over for a moment. “All right, let’s take it.”

“Wonderful! I’ll see you in two days then!”

“Yeah, see you.”

◆◇◆◇◆

“Hey! What time do you think it is?! You’re late!” shouted a small figure standing with their arms crossed at the wagon staging area.

“Don’t tell me that brat’s our client?” I muttered to Augurey.

He gave me an apologetic smile. “That’s him. Remember when I said he’s a little different? He’s just a bit cheeky. But that’s all. Yep.”

I didn’t miss that Augurey’s eyes had gotten kind of shifty, and I began to get a bad feeling.

“Augurey...” I started.

“Hey, come on, it’ll be fine. Probably. And the pay’s good.”

“The pay being good just makes me more suspicious.” I sighed. “But we’ve taken the job, so there’s no turning back now. I guess I just have to grin and bear it, whatever it is.”

“That’s the Rentt I know.” Augurey turned away to face the kid who was approaching us. “Hey! Over here! You’re, erm...”

“Vitez! Vitez Normorte!”

“Vitez, got it. You remember me from our first meeting, right? This here’s Rentt Faina, another adventurer. We’ll be your guards for this trip.”

Vitez looked at us doubtfully. “Only the two of you? I dunno...”

“We’re more capable than we look. Both of us are Bronze-class, you see. If it’s just the next town over, we’ll be fine.”

“Yeah? Okay then. Well, I’ll be napping in the wagon, so the rest is up to you.”

“Sure thing.”

Vitez left us and hopped up and into the wagon.

As I watched him, I said, “So, I guess we’ll take turns at the reins and being lookout?”

“Unless you’ve got any better ideas.”

“No. Let’s get to it.”

◆◇◆◇◆

“So what’s this all about?” I asked.

“Don’t look at me,” Augurey said. “I haven’t a clue.”

We were currently examining the ten or so bandits who’d surrounded our wagon. It wasn’t uncommon for bandits to terrorize the roads, of course, but the problem was that the ones we’d encountered seemed organized. In short, it felt as though somebody had hired them.

“Whatever the case, we can’t avoid a fight,” Augurey said. “At least they don’t look all too strong.”

“Let’s hope you’re right.” I sighed. “I’ll go. You keep an eye on Vitez.”

“Got it. Vitez! Stay in the wagon, okay?!”

I dashed off toward the bandits. After a brief few exchanges, I realized that Augurey had been right—none of them were any stronger than Iron-class, if they had been adventurers. Maybe they actually had been at one point. It was common for people at their level to grow frustrated with their lack of progress and turn to darker methods of earning coin.

Part of me was sympathetic—the same thing could happen to me one day—but that didn’t mean I was going to go easy on them. I took them down, one by one, but just as I was about to deal with the last...

KRA-KOOM!

He was struck by a bolt of lightning.

“The heck?!” I exclaimed.

The sky, which had been clear all day so far, was now full of thunderclouds. Lightning struck our surroundings incessantly, but the strangest thing was that it seemed to avoid the area around the wagon. I ran over and joined Augurey.

“What’s going on?!” I asked. “Is this what you were hiding?!”

“Well...” Augurey paused. “Okay, yes. See, Vitez has a bit of a strange ability. He summons storms when he gets agitated.”

“So this is his doing?” I turned to the boy in question. “Hey. Vitez.”

“Wh-Wh-What?!” Vitez was crying and looked terrified. “What happened to the bandits?! Are they gone?!”

“Yeah, they’re all gone,” I said gently. “Just calm down, okay? You know, with a storm like that, we would’ve been fine against a hundred bandits.”

“R-Really? They’re really gone?! Th-Thank goodness...”

The sounds of thunder and lightning began to gradually fade away. After we all took a short breather, Vitez began to talk.

“I...shouldn’t be near mom and dad,” he said. “You saw what I did. I could hurt them.”

“I wouldn’t go that far,” I said. “It looked like it avoided the area around you, after all. Have you damaged your town with it before?”

Vitez looked surprised, like he hadn’t expected my question. “Not the town itself,” he said, “but I have accidentally summoned thunderclouds around it.”

“That was the recent big storm, right?”

“Yeah...”

“Then don’t worry about it.”

“Huh?”

“You won’t summon them if you just don’t get worked up, right? It might be worth practicing a way to control it too.” I thought for a moment, then grumbled, “I kind of wish I had a power like yours, to be honest...”

Vitez looked at me in disbelief, like it had shocked him that I wasn’t really bothered. It seemed like a weight had been lifted from his shoulders. “What’s with this guy?” he asked Augurey.

“He’s always complaining about how he doesn’t have any talent, so he’s envious of you,” Augurey said. “That’s just how Rentt is.”

“Envious...of me?” Vitez let out a small laugh. “Now that’s funny. Still, finding a way to control it, huh? Maybe I’ll try that.”

“That’s the spirit,” Augurey said. “If anything happens, get in touch with us. We’ll do what we can.”

“He’s right,” I said. “Don’t hesitate to come calling.”

Vitez looked between the two of us. “Thank you.”

After that, we escorted Vitez home under the clear blue sky.

In the years that followed, the thunderclouds never became as violent again, so we knew that Vitez must have learned how to control his ability.
