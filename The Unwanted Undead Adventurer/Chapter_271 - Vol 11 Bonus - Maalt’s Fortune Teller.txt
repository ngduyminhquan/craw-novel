Vol 11 Bonus - Maalt’s Fortune Teller

“You. Yes, you there, with the mask!”

I’d been wandering through Maalt after spending some time drinking at a tavern when a sudden voice called out to me. After turning to see who it was, I was greeted with the sight of a suspicious-looking individual who appeared to be selling some kind of service.

“Me?” I asked on a whim.

People such as these almost never had anything decent to offer, but I figured there was no harm in listening to what they had to say every now and then. Even if they tried something shady, they wouldn’t be the type of person capable of doing anything to me. That wasn’t me being arrogant; it was the simple truth. If they had that kind of skill in the first place, they’d make a better living as adventurers rather than trying to pass themselves off as fortune tellers.

“Yes, you,” the stranger said. “A peculiar star shines upon you...”

That was probably the truth, in all honesty, but that did nothing to hinder my suspicion. Anybody could have guessed that these days just by looking at me.

“Pretty much,” I said. “I take it you’re a fortune teller?”

“Indeed I am.”

“I’m not going to pay you, you know.”

It was best to make clear that there was no point in pandering to me from the beginning.

However, strangely, the fortune teller shook their head and said, “No, that won’t be necessary. If I may ask for but one thing, it is that you be careful. An ill omen hangs over you on the morrow.”

“Sorry, what? If you’re not charging, then why are you... Actually, did you say ‘ill omen’?”

“Indeed. Although, in light of your previous encounters, it is nothing particularly harrowing.”

“Who are you? I mean, I know you’re probably just making this all up, but...”

“If that is what you choose to believe, then I mind not. I am simply providing you with a warning. Ah, and a few more things. Should you meet with trouble, you may find what you seek at the fountain plaza. The lost item is on the end of a shelf in a room. Also, it will rain. Now then, it is time for me to close up shop.”

“Huh? Wait, hold on just— What?”

After their quick burst of warnings, the fortune teller vanished into thin air, like mist, before I could finish what I’d been about to say. Not a trace of them remained.

Left behind alone, all I could do was mutter to myself.

“Don’t tell me they were the real deal...?”

◆◇◆◇◆

The next morning, when I walked into the living room, Lorraine was restlessly moving about.

“What’s wrong?” I asked.

“Oh, it’s just that I suddenly felt like rereading a book I read two years ago,” Lorraine explained. “The problem is, I don’t know where I put it.”

Remembering what the fortune teller had told me yesterday, I said, “I think it’s on the end of the shelf in your room.”

“What? How would you know that? Then again, that does seem likely... I’ll go check.”

Lorraine returned to her room, but barely any time passed before she came dashing back out. “How did you know?!” she exclaimed. “It was on the end of the shelf! Buried at the back too!”

Judging from what she was saying and the book she was holding in her hand, it appeared that she’d found what she’d been looking for.

“Maybe it would’ve been better if it hadn’t been there...” I muttered.

“Of course not. I want to read it. What’s wrong? Did something happen?”

Lorraine had good intuition. I told her about the fortune teller from yesterday.

“A strange fortune teller, hmm?” she said. “Those types do show up in Maalt every now and then. That’s just the kind of mood this place has. We have people like you and Laura, after all.”

I paused. “When you put it like that, an accurate fortune teller doesn’t seem like such a big deal anymore.”

“Depends on how accurate. If they’re never wrong, they’d be fairly ridiculous. Forget fortune-telling—that’s just outright precognition, something impossible for regular magic to do. It appears they were correct about my lost book, at the very least.”

“I guess.” I thought about it for a moment. “Still, they said the ill omen hanging over me today wouldn’t be as bad as the stuff I encountered in the past. I’ll probably be fine.”

“That’s a real easygoing attitude... But I suppose you’re right, if they were telling the truth. Whatever the case, be careful today.”

“Sure.”

◆◇◆◇◆

“So, how was it?”

That was the first thing out of Lorraine’s mouth when I returned home later that day. I recalled the events that had befallen me.

“Well, about that ill omen...” I began.

“Did something dangerous happen?”

“As I was walking through town, I got water splashed onto me five times.”

Lorraine’s tone was carefully neutral. “Oh?”

“Not to mention the falling flowerpot, the thief who slammed into me as he was running away, and all the other stuff...”

Lorraine burst into laughter. “Of course, it’s definitely all the ill omen’s fault!”

“As for the fountain thing,” I continued, “you know how it rained pretty hard earlier in the evening?”

“Mmm, it was bucketing down.”

“Well, it all went into the canals. I saw a kid playing nearby fall in, and I had to jump in to save him before it could turn into a flash flood.”

“That sounds like you. Was he okay?”

“Thankfully. He’d passed out from taking in too much water, but he came to and started breathing again once I got him out.”

“That’s a relief. So what about the fountain?”

“Well, the kid said he’d gotten separated from his parents. I figured we’d try going to the fountain plaza, and they were actually there.”

“Wow. Then that means your fortune teller was right about everything.”

“Who do you think they were?”

I was feeling quite doubtful about it all. It seemed to me that the fortune teller might have had some kind of ulterior motive. Maalt was a hive of activity right now, and more than a few people had come with nefarious goals in mind.

Lorraine’s response, however, sounded optimistic. “You’re thinking, if they really were that powerful, why would they go out of their way to warn you, right?” she said. “They could just get what they wanted themselves, after all. I wouldn’t bother losing sleep over it though.”

“It doesn’t worry you?”

“Maalt’s a strange town. I can’t let every little mystery get to me. My close friend came home one day as one of the undead. What’s a fortune teller compared to that?”

When she put it like that, it was pretty convincing.

“I guess you’re right,” I said.

“More importantly, I’d like to meet that fortune teller myself, sooner or later.”

“I passed by the spot where I met them yesterday, but they were gone. Maybe they’ve already left town.”

“That’s a shame. Ah, well, Maalt never runs out of curiosities, so I suppose it’s fine.” Lorraine smiled slightly. “I doubt I’ll ever get bored here.”
